---
title: How Halloween teaches us good teamwork
date: "2026-10-29"
---

Teamwork has a lot in common with trick-or-treating.

Both take participation from people on either side of the door.

If you want candy, you have to put on a costume, head outside, and knock. You can’t sit at home expecting someone to bring it to you.

Asking for help at work takes a similar kind of effort. You have to put yourself out there, admit you need something, and start a conversation that might feel uncomfortable.

And like a Halloween costume, that discomfort doesn’t necessarily mean you’re doing it wrong.

But knocking is only half the equation.

If you want people to come to you for help or advice, you have to make your door approachable.

Nobody heads straight for the dark house with the porch light off.

At work, we sometimes say, “My door is always open,” while our reactions tell a different story.

We seem annoyed by questions. We make people feel like they should already know the answer. Then we wonder why nobody asks.

You can’t make someone knock. But you can turn on the light, make them feel welcome, and have something to offer when they arrive.

Collaboration takes both: the courage to ask and the effort to welcome the person asking.

What does your porch light look like to your team?
