---
title: No one actually knows how to use AI effectively yet
date: "2026-04-29"
---

As an engineering manager, I spend a lot of time talking about AI these days. Mostly answering questions.

- What’s the best way to use it?
- What does “good” use of AI actually look like?
- How do I keep improving while everyone around me is moving faster?
- Does everything I’ve learned still matter?
- What should I even be learning now?

The questions never stop.

If you’re in any kind of leadership role, you’re probably getting the same ones. And you’re expected to have answers. Not just for yourself, but for the people looking to you for direction.

I’ll let you in on a little secret: No one knows the answers yet.

We’re in the middle of one of the most disruptive shifts our industry has seen, in a field that’s barely had time to settle before changing again. You’d think this master’s degree in machine learning would make this easier. It doesn’t.

Modern software engineering, depending on how you count it, is what, 50 to 70 years old? Compare that to medicine with a few hundred years of structured practice. Or architecture with a few thousand years of accumulated knowledge.

They’ve had time to build foundations. To test, fail, refine, and stabilize.

We’re still arguing about the fundamentals.

So if you feel behind, or unsure, or like the ground keeps moving under you, that’s not in your head. The ground is actually moving. It’s changing constantly. Of course it’s hard to feel stable right now.

We forget sometimes that it’s called computer science. Not computer “we already know the answer.” So embrace the uncertainty. This is the moment to lean into experimentation, not fear.

Form a hypothesis. Try something. See what works. See what breaks. Adjust and repeat.

Progress matters more than certainty right now.

And if you do figure it all out, let me know. I have a few questions.
