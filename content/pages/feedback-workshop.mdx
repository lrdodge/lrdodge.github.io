**Better feedback. More often. From more people.**

Most teams have a feedback problem. Not because people don't care about improving, but because meaningful feedback is difficult to get.

Managers have limited time. Senior team members can only mentor so many people. Formal reviews happen infrequently, and even the most dedicated leaders can only observe a fraction of what happens on their teams.

The result? Professional growth becomes constrained by the availability of a handful of people.

**What if everyone on your team could help everyone else grow?**

## From Feedback Bottlenecks to Growth Loops

Imagine a team where people know how to give feedback that's specific, constructive, and actionable. Just as importantly, they know how to receive feedback with curiosity rather than defensiveness, and turn those observations into meaningful improvement.

Instead of waiting for a manager or senior colleague to identify opportunities for growth, team members become active participants in each other's development.

As more people develop these skills, opportunities for feedback multiply. People improve their ability to give feedback, learn from what they receive, and apply those lessons in their next interaction.

That creates a reinforcing loop where **individual growth contributes to the growth of the entire team.**

This doesn't replace the experience, perspective, or guidance of managers and senior colleagues. It makes that guidance more effective by supplementing it with something most teams are missing: frequent, useful feedback from the people they work with every day.

## What This Workshop Will Explore

This workshop is being developed around a few fundamental skills:

- **Giving useful feedback:** Moving beyond vague praise and criticism to observations people can actually act on.
- **Receiving feedback:** Developing the curiosity and openness needed to learn from different perspectives.
- **Building peer-to-peer trust:** Creating an environment where feedback is a normal part of collaboration, not something reserved for performance reviews.
- **Turning feedback into growth:** Learning how to experiment, reflect, and make meaningful changes based on what we hear.
- **Creating sustainable feedback loops:** Helping teams develop these habits without depending on a manager to facilitate every conversation.

The goal isn't simply to make people better at giving feedback. It's to help teams become better at developing themselves.

## Where Improv Meets Leadership

My experience as both an engineering leader and an improv instructor has shown me how powerful immediate, actionable feedback can be.

In improv, performers learn by making choices, observing their impact, receiving feedback, and trying again. The opportunity to experiment and improve is built directly into the practice.

I believe many of those same principles can help professional teams create more frequent opportunities for learning and development.

This workshop will explore how to bring that kind of experiential learning into the workplace through practical exercises, reflection, and collaborative feedback.
