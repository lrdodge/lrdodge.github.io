Hi, I'm Luke. I'm a software engineer turned engineering leader, an improv teacher, and a relentlessly curious person who enjoys figuring out how things work. Especially people.

I've spent nearly two decades in software engineering, working my way from building applications to leading the teams that build them. Along the way, I've learned that writing good software is only part of the challenge. The really interesting problems involve people, communication, and finding better ways to work together.

I enjoy solving complicated technical problems, but what I find most rewarding is helping other people become better problem solvers.

## Engineering & Leadership

My career has taken me through startups, growing technology companies, and established organizations. I've worked across the stack, led major technical initiatives, helped modernize legacy systems, and built teams responsible for business-critical software.

Over time, my focus has shifted from the software itself to the people and systems that make great engineering possible.

I believe good leadership is about creating an environment where people can do their best work. That means providing clarity without micromanaging, giving honest feedback, encouraging ownership, and helping engineers develop the confidence and judgment to make their own decisions.

I'm particularly passionate about professional development. I love helping people discover what they're capable of, whether that's an engineer growing into a technical leader, someone navigating a difficult stretch in their career, or a team learning how to work together more effectively.

I also believe there's a place for playfulness in serious work. Curiosity, experimentation, and a willingness to be wrong are essential to learning. Some of the best improvements I've seen started with someone asking a question that seemed a little ridiculous at first.

## A Little Improv Goes a Long Way

Outside of software engineering, I've spent years performing, teaching, and coaching improvisational theater.

What started as a creative outlet became one of the most influential parts of how I think about collaboration, communication, and learning.

Improv has taught me a lot about listening, trusting other people, embracing uncertainty, and making decisions without knowing exactly how things will turn out. It has also given me countless opportunities to help people step outside their comfort zones and discover abilities they didn't know they had.

I've brought some of those lessons into my professional life through leadership workshops, team facilitation, and coaching. But sometimes improv is just improv, and there's something wonderful about getting on stage with a group of people and creating something ridiculous together.

## Always Curious

I've never been particularly good at sticking to one interest.

I have a master's degree in computer science with a focus on machine learning and artificial intelligence. I enjoy writing, exploring new ideas, and finding unexpected connections between things that don't initially seem related.

Outside of my work in technology, I'm also a professional photographer, wandering the streets of Kansas City searching for interesting details. When I'm not behind a camera, you might find me hiking, working on a creative project, or getting lost in a new subject simply because something caught my attention.

That curiosity is a big part of why this website exists.

It's a place to share what I've learned, explore ideas I'm still working through, and occasionally discover that something I thought I understood was a lot more complicated than I expected.
